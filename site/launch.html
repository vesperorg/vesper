Vesper · VESP · launch plan

Fair launch. No presale. No premine.

Nobody can buy Vesper before it exists, including the people building it. Block 1 is mined in public, by anyone who shows up. Development is paid for by a fixed slice of every block reward, written into the rules and visible to everyone, for four years and then never again.

Where the code is
Phases 1 to 4 built
Receivers hidden. Amounts hidden. Senders hidden in a ring of 48, three times Monero's. The first broadcast of a payment comes from a relay, not the sender, and that leak is measured and published. Node v0.9.1, 46 self-test checks. Not audited.
Public testnet
testnet-1, open
Open since 23 October 2026. Test coins (tVESP, addresses start tvsp2) with no value, ever, on a real network, so anyone can try to break it before it matters. Its genesis is tied to a Bitcoin block and its first seed node is live; a fresh node finds the network by itself. The Join page has the steps.
Mainnet genesis
No date, on purpose
Gated by the public testnet, an independent cryptographic audit, and legal advice. A date printed before those would be a promise the code cannot keep. When one exists, it appears here first.
The launch rules

Five rules, all of them enforced by the code, not by promises.

RULE 1

Zero coins before block 1

The genesis block has no outputs. There is no founder allocation, no treasury, no early round. The first coin that exists is the first mining reward.

RULE 2

Anyone mines from the first block

The same node software everyone downloads is what mines block 1. Seed-node addresses are published in advance so the network starts as a crowd, not as one computer.

RULE 3

10% of every block to the development fund

For the first 4,204,800 blocks, four years at a 30-second block, each block reward splits 90% to the miner and 10% to the fund. Transaction fees go to the miner in full. Block 4,204,801 pays miners 100%, forever.

RULE 4

Every fund payment is auditable

The fund's share lands at a one-time key like any payment, but the secret that derives it is published in the block. Anyone can verify the fund received exactly its share and not a coin more. Miners who pay it less, or pay someone else, mine an invalid block. The fund is a glass box in both directions: its income is public by construction, and it spends by naming its outputs in the open with an ordinary signature, so its spending is public on chain too. Only what it pays out to is sealed, like any payment.

RULE 5

The fund can only receive, never mint

The fund has no special powers. It cannot create coins, freeze coins, or change rules. Its only income is the 10% slice, and that slice has an end date in the code.

Economics

Set with the genesis block, not before.

Mainnet's economics are decided on paper and published here first, but they become rules only with the genesis block and the final code, so they match what was actually tested. Testnet-1 keeps demo settings, and any supply figure you see for it is not Vesper's. Planned for mainnet (decided 23 October 2026, not live): a smooth emission curve that pays each block 1/4,194,304 of the 18 million VESP not yet issued, about 4.29 VESP per 30-second block at first with half of the curve issued after about 2.8 years; then, from about year 13, a tail of 0.15 VESP per block that steps down by 0.01 every six years to 0.05 at about year 73, and 0.05 forever. No hard cap, but nearly fixed: about 21.4 million at year 43, 25.4 million at year 100, 28 million at year 150, with inflation about 0.2% from year 74 and falling. The tail exists so miners are always paid and the chain is always defended; a hard cap with no tail leaves a chain undefended once rewards end. Block time stays 30 seconds unless testnet-1 shows too many orphaned blocks, in which case 45 or 60. The numbers that are already rules:

How the coins come into existence

Nothing is mined before genesis. Not by us, not by anyone.

Testnet-1 is the rehearsal of exactly this procedure, with coins that are worth nothing: its genesis block names a Bitcoin block mined minutes before the launch moment, its starting difficulty comes from measured test mining, and anyone can check both from the chain itself.

Genesis is block 0, the first block of the real chain. It is computed from the published rules: every consensus setting (reward, halving, ring size, proof of work, the fund's address) is hashed into it, so every computer that ships with the same rules starts from the identical block, and it contains no coins. Every chain running today is a test chain that gets thrown away. On genesis day, anyone who runs the node and presses mine starts earning block rewards with their own computer, at the lowest difficulty the chain will ever have. That is the only way VESP is ever created, and the people building it start the same morning as everyone else, with the same software.

WHAT MINING IS

Electricity in, coins out

A miner's computer runs a memory-hungry puzzle, Argon2id with 32 MB per try, about 90 tries a second per laptop core and 300 to 420 a second on eight cores, until one answer is small enough. No money is deposited anywhere. The cost is the computer's time and power; the reward is the block's coins, paid to a one-time key only that miner can spend.

WHO MINES

Whoever shows up

Rewards are shared in proportion to mining power. One laptop among ten laptops earns about a tenth. The puzzle is chosen so Bitcoin's mining chips cannot run it at all; graphics cards keep an edge over CPUs, and the algorithm is an interim choice, with RandomX the likely production one, settled before any public network.

WHAT WE COMMIT TO

No secret head start

A private mining period before the public genesis is called an instamine. It is what killed the coin Monero was forked from, and it is detectable from block timestamps and difficulty. Vesper's genesis time is announced in advance and the chain's own record proves it was honoured. Three mechanics make a head start impossible rather than merely forbidden: the genesis message must contain something nobody could know earlier (for example the newest Bitcoin block ID at the launch moment), the genesis timestamp is the launch moment itself, and the starting difficulty is set from the test network's measured mining rate, not from zero.

MINING TOGETHER

One pool, with nobody running it

Decided 27 October 2026; the share-chain rules and the payout split are built and tested since 29 October (pool.py, one self-test check), not yet running in the node. One ordinary computer wins a block rarely and at random, so small miners pool their work to be paid steadily. An ordinary pool is a company that builds every block, holds the payouts and learns every member's address; a handful of them end up deciding which payments get in. Vesper's pool is part of the node itself, after Monero's P2Pool: members mine a side chain of "shares" (about one every ten seconds), and when any member finds a real block, its reward pays everyone who contributed shares in the last six hours, straight to their own wallets at one-time keys, with no balance held by anyone and no addresses on the share chain. Members build their own blocks, so no pool can leave payments out. Honest line: the protocol cannot make an ordinary pool impossible; this one is built in, the easiest way to mine together, and the only one Vesper's software and community support. It needs a reward with hundreds of outputs, which changes block 0, so it is settled before mainnet.

WHERE IT RUNS

Every node, not one PC

The chain lives on every computer running the software. For testnet-1 the Vesper team runs one seed node on a rented server (never a home connection) so strangers can find each other; the kit is in deploy/, and whoever runs a seed node does not run the network and cannot stop it. Honest status: there is no foundation yet (a legal entity is on the pre-genesis list). The node now has per-address limits, a one-hour ban for peers that break the rules, a peer table that treats gossip as candidates to be checked, and connection timeouts; all of it tested in simulation and on one PC, none of it against real strangers yet. The public testnet is that test. Nothing between nodes is encrypted; --proxy sends a node's traffic through Tor.

How to help before genesis

There is nothing to buy. There is plenty to do.

Run a testnet-1 node

Install, start the node, mine tVESP that is worth nothing. The seed node's address is on the Join page the day the server is up; until then the same software runs a chain of its own on your PC. What you give is a real network to test against.

Try to trace a payment

The code is readable on purpose. Every phase ships with a test that states what it still leaks. Find a leak the test does not list and the fund pays you for it.

Review the math

If you know elliptic curves, ring signatures or zero-knowledge proofs, read curve.py and privacy.py and say what is wrong. Before genesis, that is the most valuable hour anyone can give.

Donate to the fund after genesis

The fund's address is announced with the genesis block. Donations to it are ordinary payments and show in the public ledger above. Nothing is accepted before that.

Read this. Vesper has no token, no presale and no sale of any kind, now or at launch. Testnet-1's tVESP are test coins with no value, and anyone offering tVESP or VESP for money is not us. The only way to get VESP on day one, if a real network ever exists, is to mine it.
Questions

Asked already.

Why no presale? You could raise money faster.

Two reasons. Privacy-coin communities treat any pre-launch allocation as a sign the coin exists to enrich insiders, and they are usually right. And selling a privacy coin to the public is the most heavily scrutinised version of a token sale there is. A mined launch with a visible fund avoids both and is how the privacy coins people actually trust were born.

Isn't a development fund just a slow premine?

A premine is coins that exist before anyone else can earn any. The fund earns alongside miners, from the same blocks, at a rate everyone can see, and it stops. Zcash funded itself this way; so do several chains since. The difference from a premine is the three words "visible", "capped" and "ends".

Can the fund be changed later to take more?

Only by changing the consensus rules, which means every node operator choosing to run new software. The fund cannot do it alone, and a change that raises the fund's share would be visible to everyone before it took effect.

Is it untraceable today?

No, and this page will not say so until it is. Today receivers and amounts are hidden and the sender is hidden among 48 outputs, Monero's design with three times its ring, which is still a crowd of 48 and not of everyone. Phase 4 means the first broadcast of a payment comes from a relay rather than the sender's node, and the leak is measured: an attacker running 10% of the nodes still names the sender's node about 11% of the time. Nothing between nodes is encrypted unless you run through Tor. Hiding the sender among every output is research, and the code has not been independently audited. Genesis comes after the testnet and that audit, not before.

Who is behind it?

A pseudonymous developer, Vesper, building in the open with an AI pair. The name on the code is the project, not a person. Which is exactly why the fund's income is on chain, the spend log is public, and the audit comes before the launch.
